`journey verify` now explains a reloadThen-style end-state check that holds while the checks beside it fail (#817). End-state checks are judged one by one, so a check that held under a mutation was not blocked by the checks that failed beside it, and relaxing them would change nothing. The targeted refusal now says that, names every check that failed, and says when the held check is an absence check, which also holds on a page or view that has not loaded. `journey verify --plan` (and `--plan --json`, as `isolationNotes`) flags that shape before any browser opens: an end-state absence check that shares its mutations with other end-state checks. Verdicts are unchanged: such a check stays a `cascade` and the proof stays `inconclusive`. To prove it, first wait for content that shows the view loaded, for example by reopening the view in a later step.

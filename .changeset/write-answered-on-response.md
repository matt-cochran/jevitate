`journey verify --mutate` now treats a write as answered once its body has reached the browser. A POST answered with `Cache-Control: no-store` whose body the page does not read never reports that it finished, so a proof read `inconclusive` ("a write still unanswered") about a request answered in milliseconds; those proofs can now reach a verdict. A response alone does not answer a write: a server that sends its headers and keeps working has its write kept open up to the replay's bound, and the proof still reads `inconclusive`. A body that has arrived gets 2s more, and a write answered and then aborted, or dropped with its document, gets 2s too. A page that reads a streaming body whose first bytes have arrived reads as answered after those 2s, a limit stated in `docs/outcomes.md`, and an unread `no-store` body over about 2 MB stays `inconclusive`. A block-write now also notices its write being answered after the block when the body is unread. `journey run`: a `responseStatus` check on such a request now reads its status instead of failing with "still awaiting a response"; a gRPC-web or Connect response still waits for its body, since its result can be in the trailer.
